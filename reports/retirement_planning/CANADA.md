# Canadian Retirement, Pension & Tax Jurisdiction Rules

This reference document defines the statutory formulas, pension mechanisms, and provincial tax rules applicable to Canadian tax residents (specifically Ontario) during accumulation and decumulation.

---

## 1. Statutory Government Pensions

### 1.1 Canada Pension Plan (CPP & Enhanced CPP2)
- **Contributory Period**: From age 18 to age 65 (47 maximum years), with a 17% general drop-out provision (~8 lowest years dropped), leaving a 39-year baseline denominator.
- **Base CPP**: Replaces 25% of average career earnings up to the Year's Maximum Pensionable Earnings (YMPE).
- **Enhanced CPP (CPP1 & CPP2)**:
  - Phase 1 (2019–2023): Increases income replacement from 25% to 33.33% up to the YMPE.
  - Phase 2 (2024+): Applies an additional 4% employee + 4% employer contribution on earnings between the YMPE and the higher Year's Additional Maximum Pensionable Earnings (YAMPE, ~114% of YMPE).
- **Actuarial Adjustments (Claim Timing)**:
  - Age 65: 100% baseline benefit.
  - Early claim (Age 60 to 64): Reduced by **0.6% per month** before age 65 (maximum -36% at age 60).
  - Delayed claim (Age 65 to 70): Increased by **0.7% per month** after age 65 (maximum **+42%** at age 70).
- **Indexation**: Adjusted annually on January 1st to match 100% of CPI inflation.

### 1.2 Old Age Security (OAS)
- **Eligibility & Residency Proration**: Full OAS requires 40 years of Canadian residency after age 18. Partial OAS is calculated as $\frac{N}{40}$ of full entitlement for individuals with $10 \le N < 40$ years of residency.
- **Deferral Incentive**: OAS can be deferred up to age 70 for a **+0.6% per month** bonus (maximum **+36%** at age 70).
- **Age 75 Enhancement**: Automatic 10% increase in the baseline OAS pension starting the month following an individual's 75th birthday.
- **OAS Recovery Tax (Clawback)**:
  - Threshold is indexed annually to CPI (~$93,200 CAD in 2026).
  - Income exceeding the threshold (Line 23600 Net Income) is clawed back at a **15%** marginal rate until the benefit is fully eliminated.

### 1.3 Guaranteed Income Supplement (GIS)
- Income-tested non-taxable benefit for low-income seniors.
- Subject to a steep **50% clawback** on net income (excluding OAS).

## 2. Income Tax Mechanics (Ontario & Federal)

### 2.1 Tax Brackets & Basic Personal Amount (BPA)
- All federal and provincial tax brackets and the Basic Personal Amount (BPA) shift upwards each tax year by 100% of the annual CPI inflation rate.

### 2.2 Account Types & Tax Regimes
- **Tax-Free Savings Account (TFSA)**:
  - Contributions are made with after-tax dollars; growth and withdrawals are 100% tax-free.
  - New annual contribution room is indexed to inflation and rounded to the nearest $500.
  - Unused room carries forward indefinitely; withdrawn amounts are re-added to room on January 1st of the following calendar year.
- **Registered Retirement Savings Plan (RRSP) / Registered Retirement Income Fund (RRIF)**:
  - Contributions are tax-deductible against marginal income; growth is tax-deferred; all withdrawals are taxed as regular ordinary income.
  - Must be converted to a RRIF by December 31st of the year the account holder turns age 71.
  - Mandatory minimum withdrawal schedules apply starting at age 71 according to CRA age-based percentage tables.
- **Non-Registered / Taxable Account**:
  - Capital gains feature a 50% inclusion rate (or statutory threshold rules).
  - Eligible Canadian dividends receive gross-up and dividend tax credits.
- **Carrying Charges / Investment Loan Interest Deduction**:
  - Interest incurred on money borrowed to earn investment income is fully deductible on Line 22100 against ordinary income.

## 3. Strategic Tax Optimization Protocols

1. **RRSP Meltdown Strategy**: Systematic withdrawals in early retirement (prior to OAS/CPP/RRIF activation) to utilize bottom tax brackets and avoid punitive RRIF minimums post-71.
2. **TFSA Reinvestment Transfer**: Reinvesting excess proceeds from the RRSP meltdown into newly indexed annual TFSA contribution room to lock in lifetime tax exemption.
3. **Spousal Pension Income Splitting (Form T1036)**: Post-age 65, up to 50% of eligible RRIF and pension income can be allocated to the lower-earning spouse to equalize tax brackets and avoid OAS clawback.
